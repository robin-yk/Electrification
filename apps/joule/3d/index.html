Cartesian volume mesh; curved boundaries are stair-stepped. Single solid with end electrodes. Gas flow, electrical contact resistance and CAD import are outside this draft.

What this draft solves

A three-dimensional solid is divided into Cartesian cells. Each cell has an electric potential and temperature. The calculation runs in a Web Worker and can be cancelled. Every press of Calculate solves the current inputs.

∇ · J = 0,   J = −σ(T)∇φ,   qJ = J · (−∇φ)
ρm Cp ∂T/∂t = ∇ · (k∇T) + qJ

Electrical calculation

The solver first applies a unit voltage. Interior face conductances use the series resistance of adjacent half-cells. A and B are ideal equipotential terminals on the −Z and +Z ends; all other surfaces are electrically insulated. The unit solution is scaled to the selected setpoint and voltage, current and power limits. Resistor power is assigned to the adjacent cells in proportion to their half-cell resistance, including terminal half-cells. Total heat therefore matches terminal electrical power to the linear solver tolerance.

Thermal calculation

Interior conduction uses kA/Δx. Exposed surfaces exchange heat with a uniform ambient through h(Ts−Ta) + εσSB(Ts⁴−Ta⁴). Surface temperature is solved with the half-cell conduction resistance. Electrode patches exchange heat with the specified sink through a thermal conductance; ambient convection and radiation do not also act on those patches. This is radiation to a large uniform environment; exchange and reflection between different solid surfaces are not solved.

Steady and transient calculations iterate temperature and electric resistivity together. Transient heat storage uses backward Euler, with each step ending at pulse transitions. Pulse amplitude is the supply setpoint, and the off value is zero. Density, Cp, k and emissivity are constant. The resistivity law is linear about 25 °C; a nonpositive value or a temperature above the specified ceiling stops calculation. Nonconvergence establishes no steady solution.

Reading results

  • Temperatures and potentials are cell-centred. The displayed current magnitude is reconstructed from face currents; cell Joule heat uses the conservative resistor powers.
  • The colour range uses the entire solved solid, including cells hidden by a cut. Clicking a visible cell reports its coordinates and value.
  • Transient scalar outputs describe the final time interval. A run ending during an off interval can have zero final power while the solid remains hot. dTavg/dt is the last interval's volume-average temperature change; it is zero at steady state.
  • The heat balance includes supplied power, ambient heat transfer, electrode heat transfer and transient storage. Numerical balance is not experimental validation.
  • Changing a physical input clears the old field. Refine the mesh and time step to check the quantities you will use; thin walls and electrode patches may require a finer grid.

Scope and material inputs

One homogeneous solid; predefined shapes; ideal end terminals; DC or electrically quasistatic pulses. No gas energy or flow equation, pressure drop, electrical contact resistance, multi-part CAD assembly, surface-to-surface radiation, stress, skin effect, reaction or phase change is included. Preset values are illustrative inputs, not validated material curves. Cp and emissivity are explicit example assumptions. Material grade, porosity, reduction state and temperature range need measured data before experimental prediction.
